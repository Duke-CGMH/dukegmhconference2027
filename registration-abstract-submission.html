Abstract Submission

The Duke Center for Global Mental Health is excited to invite you to submit an abstract for the third Duke Global Mental Health Conference on February 19th, 2027, at Penn Pavilion on Duke University’s West Campus. Our theme is: “Shared Learning, Creative Solutions: Global Mental Health in Partnership”. This event will bring together leaders, researchers, practitioners, and advocates from the Duke community and our partners around the world to share our work and ideas for working together.

We invite submissions related to global mental health research, practice, education, or policy. Presentation formats include virtual posters, in-person posters, and oral presentations. We encourage collaborative submissions that include global and/or community-based colleagues. Abstracts can be programmatic (do not need to be research-focused), and they will be evaluated based on their relevance to a broad range of partners and potential for impact in the areas of global mental health research, practice, or policy. We look forward to receiving your submissions and exploring new opportunities to advance mental health through shared learning and global collaboration.

Abstract submission deadline: Friday, December 4, 2026, at 11:59 p.m. EST.
For questions, please contact dukegmh@duke.edu.

Presentation Categories

Please select your preferred presentation format when submitting your abstract. Presentation assignments may depend on the number and scope of submissions.

In-Person Poster Presentation

Poster presentations provide an opportunity to visually communicate the key elements, findings, or lessons learned from a research project or program. Posters will be displayed throughout the conference, with a designated session for presenters to discuss their work with attendees. Poster size: Posters must not exceed 36×48 inches wide.

Presenter responsibilities: Presenters must bring a printed poster to the conference, set it up before the first conference session, attend the designated poster session, and remove it after the final conference session.

Virtual Poster

Virtual posters are intended for participants who are unable to attend the conference in person. Accepted virtual posters will be displayed on video screens throughout Penn Pavilion during the poster session and conference transitions.

Submission deadline: Accepted virtual posters must be emailed in PPTX or PNG format to dukegmh@duke.edu by Friday, February 5, 2027.

Oral Presentation

Oral presentations will provide an opportunity to share about research or practice, policy, or educational initiatives in global mental health. Presentations should be focused, engaging, and accessible to our diverse audience. Up to three people can speak during a presentation, especially to accommodate projects where both researchers and partners are in attendance to contribute. 10-minute or 20-minute formats will be assigned based on scope and number of presenters.

All oral presenters must attend the conference in-person and have an active affiliation or partnership with Duke or another university in the Research Triangle.

Abstract Submission Guidelines

Please upload an abstract as a Word document or PDF. Abstracts should follow the structure below:

  • Background: Provide brief context for the project and describe its primary objective or purpose.
  • Methods: Describe the study design, setting, participants, and analytic approach, as applicable.
  • Results: Summarize key findings and outcomes. If your study is still in progress, please include preliminary results.
  • Discussion: Explain the implications of the work and its relevance to global mental health research, practice, education, or policy.

Word Limit: 250 words. Title, author names, and affiliations do not count toward the word limit.

Formatting and Review Requirements

Submit text only. Do not include tables, figures, or references. Include the abstract title along with author names and affiliations.

Abstracts may describe research, programmatic initiatives, or practice- and policy-oriented work; submissions do not need to be research-focused. Please ensure your abstract clearly communicates the purpose and significance of your work and its potential relevance to the broader global mental health community.

We look forward to reviewing your submissions and welcoming you to the 2027 Duke Global Mental Health Conference!
